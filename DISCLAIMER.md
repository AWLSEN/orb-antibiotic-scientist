# Disclaimer

## What this project produces

This repository hosts an autonomous AI agent that generates and computationally evaluates **candidate molecules** intended to bind bacterial drug targets (initially MRSA DNA gyrase B).

The output of this agent consists of:

- SMILES strings (for small molecules) and FASTA sequences (for peptides)
- Predicted 3D binding poses
- Computed physicochemical properties (Lipinski, Veber, Ghose, SA Score)
- Computed predicted binding affinity (AutoDock Vina ΔG, DiffDock poses)
- Computed ADMET predictions (SwissADME, ProTox-II, pkCSM)
- Computed novelty assessments (ChEMBL Tanimoto similarity)
- Generated mechanism hypotheses and literature reviews

## What this project is NOT

These candidates have **not** been:

- Synthesized in a laboratory
- Tested in any biological assay (minimum inhibitory concentration, bacterial growth, etc.)
- Tested in any cell-based or animal model
- Reviewed or approved by any regulatory body (FDA, EMA, etc.)
- Evaluated by a licensed medical professional

The outputs of this project are **computational hypotheses only.** They are intended as starting points for experimental medicinal chemistry research — not as therapies, treatments, or medical advice.

## Not medical advice

Nothing produced by this repository constitutes medical, pharmacological, or clinical advice. Do not use any information from this project to make decisions about your health. If you are sick, see a licensed physician.

## Not therapeutic claims

We make no claim that any candidate produced by this agent will be safe or effective in humans, animals, or any biological system. Historically, fewer than 1 in 10,000 computational drug candidates reach approved-drug status. The appropriate interpretation of a high-ranking candidate on the leaderboard is *"worth experimental follow-up,"* not *"will work."*

## Scientific scope

- The agent's reasoning is generated by a large language model and may contain errors of fact, chemistry, or biology.
- All binding-affinity predictions are derived from physics-based scoring functions (AutoDock Vina) or machine-learning models (DiffDock, GNINA), which have known systematic biases and false-positive rates.
- Novelty checks are performed against snapshotted versions of ChEMBL and DrugBank and do not cover patent literature, trade-secret libraries, or unpublished work.
- Retrosynthesis feasibility predictions are heuristic and do not guarantee that proposed routes are practical or cost-effective.
